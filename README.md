## **Carving Power, Sealing Eternity: A Comparative Study of Gemstone Engraving in Ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia**

---

## Abstract

This article offers a comparative study of gemstone engraving in two of the ancient world’s most influential civilizations: Egypt and Mesopotamia. Drawing upon archaeological, historical, and iconographic sources, the study examines how the art of carving on precious and semi-precious stones served as a medium for expressing power, identity, and religious belief in these two distinct cultural contexts. The analysis reveals a fundamental divergence in the function and meaning of engraved gemstones: in Mesopotamia, carved stones -particularly cylinder seals- functioned as administrative and political tools, serving as markers of authority, ownership, and bureaucratic control. In contrast, in ancient Egypt, engraved gemstones were primarily used as amulets and funerary objects, designed to protect the deceased and ensure their safe passage into the afterlife. By comparing these two traditions, the article argues that the differences in the use of engraved gemstones reflect broader societal and ideological distinctions between the two civilizations, one centered on statecraft and law, the other on cosmic order and spiritual continuity. This comparative approach offers new insights into the relationship between material culture, power, and belief in the ancient world, and contributes to the fields of art history, cultural studies, and ancient Near Eastern studies.
